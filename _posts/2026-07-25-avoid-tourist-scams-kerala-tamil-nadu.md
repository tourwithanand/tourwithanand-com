<h1 class="blog-title">How to Avoid Common Tourist Scams in Kerala and Tamil Nadu</h1>
</header>

<img src="/images/places-to-visit-tamilnadu-mobile.png" alt="Temple architecture in Tamil Nadu" class="blog-hero-image">

<div class="blog-content">
<p>Most visitors to Kerala and Tamil Nadu have a completely trouble-free trip. The handful who don't almost always run into the same small set of scams, and every one of them is easy to avoid once you know what to look for.</p>

<h2>1. The Overpriced "Prepaid" Taxi</h2>
<p>At busy transport hubs, unofficial touts sometimes present themselves as running an official prepaid taxi counter and quote a fare well above the real rate. The fix: agree your fare in writing, such as a WhatsApp message, before you travel, with a company you've already vetted, not on the spot.</p>

<h2>2. The "Helpful" Stranger Who Is Actually an Unofficial Guide</h2>
<p>A friendly local offers to show you around, then asks for a large tip or steers you into a shop paying them commission. A polite decline and pointing to your own prearranged driver or guide ends this quickly.</p>

<h2>3. Commission Shopping Stops</h2>
<p>Some tour drivers are paid by spice shops, gem dealers, or ayurveda stores to bring tourists in, sometimes with mild pressure to buy. Ask directly before booking: "Are there any mandatory shopping stops on this itinerary?" A transparent operator will simply say no.</p>

<h2>4. Houseboat and Hotel Booking Mismatches</h2>
<p>Photos online don't always match the boat or room you actually get, especially with last-minute bookings made through unofficial middlemen. Booking through an operator who personally inspects the boats and hotels they recommend avoids this.</p>

<h2>5. Currency and Change Scams</h2>
<p>Occasionally a vendor claims not to have change for a large note, or an exchange counter quotes a poor rate assuming you won't check. Carry some smaller denominations and use UPI or card payment where possible.</p>

"Nearly every scam depends on a stranger you'll never see again. Remove the stranger, and you remove the scam."
